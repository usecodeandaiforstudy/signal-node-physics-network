  THE QUESTION AS POSED CANNOT BE ANSWERED BY THIS DESIGN, and the reason is
  measured rather than argued:

  (1) THE COMPARISON WINDOW SCALES AWAY.  The hybrid curve's first recorded
      point is twice the per-epoch budget, and the budget grows with N (1200 at
      N=52, ... 5000 at N=208).  At 1 of 3 counts the first point
      is already past 3000, so the calibrated arm has NO MEASUREMENT there and
      the @3000 column is UNDEFINED -- not bad, absent.  **The window
      disappears before the advantage does.**

  (2) WHERE IT IS STILL DEFINED, THE ADVANTAGE IS GONE: N=104
      shows the hybrid BEHIND the search arms.  The margin at N=104 is
      -0.5593, against +0.4060 at N=52.

      MECHANISM NOT ESTABLISHED.  The hybrid's R^2 at N=104 is negative
      throughout (its first four points are -0.52, -0.53, -0.51, -0.46),
      which the base's own readout-params-per-sample rule would NOT have
      predicted -- 104/256 = 0.41x is well inside the 0.20x-safe to
      2.19x-failing band -- and N=208 does BETTER than N=104 while being
      larger still.  **So this is not a clean monotone capacity story, and
      the collapse is not explained here.**

  (3) THE CONFOUND FLAGGED IN ADVANCE IS REAL AND NOW PRICED: N changes the
      per-epoch budget (600 -> 1200 -> 2500) as well as the geometry, so this
      design varies three things at once.  **A budget-matched variant -- a FIXED
      per-epoch budget across N -- is what would answer the question**, and it
      is not run here.  The honest close of section 11's tail is therefore that
      the question needs restating before it can be answered.
