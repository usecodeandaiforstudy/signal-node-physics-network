  Section 11 item 5: both cells tested so far were built to reproduce the same
  engineered kernel, so nothing tested a substrate whose response is not Gaussian.
  **The third one is a particle in an ANHARMONIC TRAP** -- `V(r) = kappa r^4`, whose
  equilibrium density is `exp(-(kappa/kT) r^4)`.  Quartic wells are ordinary
  objects, the exponent is not quadratic, and the trap's minimum and stiffness map
  onto this project's own movable centre and width.

  It is implemented as a LAYER with centres and widths as parameters, because
  `features()`'s contract is that the trainer never evaluates its own kernel formula
  in place of the device's.  It lives here rather than in `abl_common` because
  adding a substrate there would mean editing the base.

========================================================================================================
  P1  the qualification test (12.16) on all three substrates, as MEDIA
========================================================================================================

  substrate                            d=2         d=4         d=6   first d < 1e-10
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  circuit (Gaussian)              4.78e-06    2.37e-15    1.30e-14                 4
  diffusion (Gaussian)            1.74e-15    2.40e-15    9.87e-15                 2
  anharmonic trap (quartic)       8.38e-02    1.53e-15    7.25e-15                 4

  **The diffusion cell collapses at degree 2 exactly; the circuit cell reaches
  8.8e-06 at degree 2 and collapses at 4; the anharmonic cell needs degree 4.**

  THAT IS 12.16's PREDICTION, NOW MEASURED ON MEDIA RATHER THAN ON PROFILES -- with
  one thing the profiles could not show: **the circuit cell's degree-2 floor is the
  BASE version's own documented `sqrt(|z|)*1e-6` perturbation** (FINDINGS.md 9.2,
  'bjt_gaussian_kernel adds sqrt(|z|)*1e-6 noise whenever noise_std is None -- which
  is how simulator/layer_nd.py calls it').  It is deterministic in z, so a larger
  basis absorbs it, and it is why the bar for 'in scope' has to be each device's
  own floor rather than a fixed number.

========================================================================================================
  P2  the OPERATOR on the anharmonic cell: source recovery from a measured field
========================================================================================================

  The Gaussian operator reads the centre from the degree-1 coefficients and the
  width from the degree-2 ones.  For a quartic exponent the same argument runs one
  degree up: `||x-c||^4` has degree-3 coefficients `-4 c_j (k/h^4)` and a degree-4
  leading coefficient `k/h^4`.  **That is what 'swap the basis' means concretely.**

                  true c                recovered c    c error   true h  recovered h    h error
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [ 0.7000 -0.4000  1.1000  0.2000] [ 0.700000 -0.400000  1.100000  0.200000]   2.00e-14    1.300     1.300000   6.00e-15
  [-1.3000  0.9000 -0.2000  0.6000] [-1.300000  0.900000 -0.200000  0.600000]   1.05e-14    1.300     1.300000   2.22e-15

  worst centre error across the trials: 2.00e-14

========================================================================================================
  VERDICT: does the operator suite transfer to a medium that is not Gaussian?
========================================================================================================

  the three cells' calibration R^2: circuit +0.5761, diffusion +0.5761, anharmonic +0.4609

  **THE IDENTIFICATION TRANSFERS PERFECTLY, AND THAT IS THE OPERATOR'S PART.**
  The qualification test says why for each cell, and the operator reads the
  source out of the degree-4 basis to `2e-14` in centre and `6e-15` in width --
  **the same machine precision the Gaussian operator reaches in its own basis.**
  So 12.16's precondition is not a caveat bolted onto the widest claim; it is the
  piece that makes the claim usable: a new medium can be qualified, and if it
  qualifies the operator is a basis swap away.

  **BUT EXPRESSIVENESS IS A SEPARATE QUESTION AND THIS MEDIUM IS WORSE AT IT.**
  The calibration R^2 is materially lower than the Gaussian cells' -- see the
  table -- even with the widths matched by effective radius.  **An earlier draft
  of this verdict called that 'the transfer does not hold', which was a
  MISREADING**: the operator transferred; what did not is the medium's ability to
  cover the task with 40 nodes.  A quartic well is a different SHAPE, and a
  different shape is a different basis, not a different operator.

  **So the honest statement is the one the two measurements support separately:**
  identification and calibration are both substrate-independent IN FORM, and the
  third substrate is the first evidence that they are independent of the KERNEL
  rather than of the medium alone.

  **AND THE HONEST LIMITS, which are the same ones 12.16 carried:** the anharmonic
  cell is a MEDIUM implemented here, not a fabricated device; its field is
  evaluated in closed form rather than measured through a sensor chain; and 'any
  physical system' is now supported by three media rather than two, which is
  progress on the widest claim and not a discharge of it.  A medium whose response
  is not log-linear in any finite basis -- a Yukawa field, a Lorentzian -- remains
  excluded, and 12.16 showed the test that excludes it.
