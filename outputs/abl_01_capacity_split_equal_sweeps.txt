  'hybrid' = geometry probed by FD (same budget as the geometry arm)
             + source strengths CALIBRATED in closed form.
  W_ij IS the excitation strength of particle j, so the calibration is
  an offline source-strength setting, not a numerical shortcut.
  Cost accounting for arch G: the calibration is ONE-TIME
    physical route : 13312 measurement-equivalents (n_train x sum_k N_k)
    digital twin   : 0 physical measurements
    per-epoch FD   : 408 forward passes/epoch x 40 epochs = 16320
  Compare the one-time cost against the TOTAL FD cost before
  concluding that calibrating is cheaper -- on this problem it is not;
  it is cheaper only when the epoch count or the particle count is
  large.  What it does buy unconditionally is that W is then EXACT.
