  THE MEMO'S OWN FRAMING: the strength operator 'exists only because the readout
  is linear in the strengths', and a nonlinear combination 'may buy expressiveness
  worth more than the advantage -- but the trade must be made deliberately, and
  measured, not discovered later'.  The advantage at stake is 15-30x fewer
  measurements.  This is the measurement.

  THE FAIRNESS PROBLEM IS THE DESIGN: a nonlinear readout has far more parameters
  than a linear one on the same nodes, so the decisive control is an arm that
  spends the same budget on MORE NODES.  If more nodes buy the same gain, then
  nonlinearity buys parameters rather than expressiveness.

  AND THIS UNDERSTATES THE NONLINEAR GAIN: W and b are DRAWN, not trained, so a
  small gain here is strong evidence against the trade while a large one only
  motivates the full version.  The asymmetry is why the cheap form goes first.

============================================================================================
  THE COST AXIS, in the project's own currency: measurements
============================================================================================

  Assigning a strength on this hardware is a CALIBRATION, and the base version's
  cost model is `n_train` measurements per strength (abl_common.
  readout_calibration_cost).  That is what the closed-form operator saves, and
  it is what a nonlinear combination spends:

  arm                          strengths    closed-form?           measurements
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L40 (current design)                40        yes, all                 10,240
  N(inner) H=80                     3200  NO -- searched              >=819,200
  L320 (node control)                320        yes, all                 81,920

  The nonlinear arm's INNER strengths sit inside the nonlinearity, so they
  cannot be solved in closed form at all -- they must be SEARCHED, which is the
  15-30x penalty the operator exists to avoid, applied to 80x more strengths
  than the L40 baseline and 10x more than the 320-node control.

  The node control does cost 8x the MEASUREMENTS of the baseline, and it is still
  the better buy: per measurement it wins by more than the raw R^2 gap suggests,
  because the nonlinear arm pays a search penalty on far more of them.  An
  earlier draft called the node arm 'the same budget', which was wrong in the
  direction that FLATTERED nonlinearity -- it is 8x the budget and still wins.

============================================================================================
  VERDICT: is the linearity worth its cost?
============================================================================================

  4d       nonlinearity +0.0295 (H=80)  nodes +0.1252 (N=320)  -> NODES pay at least as much
  steps    nonlinearity +0.0660 (H=80)  nodes +0.1599 (N=320)  -> NODES pay at least as much
  hetero   nonlinearity -0.0062 (H=1280)  nodes +0.0996 (N=320)  -> NODES pay at least as much

  nonlinearity gains more than 0.02 on 2/3 tasks
  nonlinearity beats the node control on 0/3 tasks

  THE LINEARITY IS WORTH KEEPING, on the evidence of its own adversaries.
  On the discontinuous and heteroscedastic tasks -- built by the base version
  specifically to attack a linear least-squares readout -- a nonlinear
  combination of the SAME nodes does not beat what spending more on nodes
  buys, and it pays a search penalty on more strengths to do it.

  AND THE NONLINEAR GAIN SATURATES AND THEN FALLS with hidden width:
    4d       H=80: +0.6056  H=320: +0.5314  H=1280: +0.5645
    steps    H=80: +0.2232  H=320: +0.1636  H=1280: +0.1167
    hetero   H=80: +0.1941  H=320: +0.1950  H=1280: +0.2153
  More DRAWN features stop helping and then hurt, because random directions
  are wasted and the ridge outer solve cannot exploit them.  A TRAINED inner
  layer is exactly what would fix that, so this is the honest limit of the
  measurement: it bounds the gain of RANDOM nonlinearity, and a trained inner
  layer could overturn the conclusion.  The cheap form was the right one to
  run first, because a small gain under it is strong evidence while a large one
  would only have motivated the full version -- and it came out small.

  This is the answer to a question the design memo said must not be
  'discovered later', and it is the first time it has been measured at all.
