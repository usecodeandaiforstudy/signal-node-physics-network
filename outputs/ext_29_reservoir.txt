  The base's section 11 item 5: on an excitable medium the only trainable part is
  the readout, which requires giving up movable centres and per-particle widths.

  This script tests exactly that.  The geometry is never trained, no probe is
  spent, and no gradient is taken: one fixed random instance plus ONE closed-form
  stagewise solve -- the same solve the base's calibration uses.

  WHAT THE RESERVOIR SAVES IS THE SEARCH, NOT THE SOLVE.  The solve still costs
  n_train * sum(N) measurements, so the reservoir's only free knob is to spend the
  search's share on REDRAWING the geometry.  That is the P2 question, at equal
  cost: R = 1 + P/S, where P is the hybrid's probe cost and S the solve cost.

  SELECTION, stated because it is where a reservoir can cheat: the redraw is
  chosen on a held-out slice of TRAINING, never on test.  Mean held-out minus
  test for the selected draw is -0.0078 on 2d/G and
  +0.0769 is what an ORACLE pick (best by TEST) would add on average.
  **The oracle is printed as a bound and is not a claim.**

  THE RESERVOIR DOES NOT HOLD UP against the arm that searches AND solves --
  but NOT for the reason item 5 implies, and the distinction is the finding:

  (a) IT IS NOT CAPACITY.  With no probes and no gradient at all, best-of-R beats
      the geometry arm -- which spends the whole probe budget -- in most cells.
      So 'only the readout is trainable' is not by itself disqualifying; what
      wins is search PLUS solve, and the reservoir has only the solve.

  (b) IT IS SELECTION.  A reservoir has one knob, REDRAWING, and a redraw has to
      be PICKED.  In about half the cells the pick is WORSE than not picking at
      all -- worse than the very first draw -- and P3 shows what fixes it: more
      validation data improves the selection, and an oracle pick would add more
      still.  **The binding constraint is model selection on few held-out
      samples, not the frozen geometry.**

  (c) AND THIS IS ext_21's FAILURE MODE IN A DIFFERENT SETTING.  There, no guard
      for the operator suite worked and selection did worse than the pool mean;
      here, redraw selection loses to no selection.  Two independent designs, one
      cause: choosing among candidates when the held-out slice is small.

  SCOPE: seven tasks, two architectures, 3 seeds, the diffusion substrate, the
  base's own effective_budget and its default closed_form_lam=1e-3.  12.13 showed
  the frontier verdict is insensitive to that lambda, so it is not a live confound
  here -- but 12.12 showed the LOW-budget comparison is not, so no low-budget
  claim is made from this run.
