==============================================================================
  P3  cost of forgetting the amplitude calibration, per rank
==============================================================================
  The base version's number for this is 9.98 uncalibrated against
  5.92e-08 calibrated in transfer error.  Here it is asked of each rank:
  an uncalibrated reading is a per-SOURCE rescaling of the features, and a
  rank-k readout has fewer degrees of freedom to absorb column distortion.

    level      k=8 dR2     k=4 dR2     k=3 dR2     k=2 dR2
  -------------------------------------------------------
     none    -219.8510   -219.9198   -219.9404   -220.5239
      low    -219.8901   -219.9580   -219.9846   -220.5348
   medium    -219.9145   -219.9727   -220.0374   -220.5522
     high    -220.7854   -220.5938   -220.6971   -221.5435

  mean damage by rank (more negative = the rank suffers more):
    k=2: -220.7886
    k=3: -220.1649
    k=4: -220.1111
    k=8: -220.1102

  The damage is the SAME for every rank to within 0.05 R^2 out of a 220 collapse, i.e. 0.02%.
  So the rank constraint neither helps nor hurts here: forgetting the
  amplitude calibration destroys the readout whatever its rank, and the
  ordering between ranks is far too small to carry a claim.  The earlier
  text in this file called that ordering a 'cost' -- it is not; it is
  noise on a collapse.  What the table DOES establish is that the base
  version's mandatory-calibration finding is rank-independent, so the
  amplitude calibration cannot be traded away for parameters.
