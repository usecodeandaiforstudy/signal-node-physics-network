  LIMITATION, measured rather than assumed: partition-of-unity normalisation
  is NOT always detectable.  With the two nodes CO-LOCATED at different
  widths the normalised profile is a genuine two-scale function and is
  caught; with the two nodes at random positions one usually dominates the
  probe region and the result is indistinguishable from that single node
  (the first version of this control used random positions and came out at
  the single-node value).  A consistency check on a NORMALISED channel
  therefore has to be told which regime it is in.  This connects to the base
  version's own finding that normalisation is what breaks parameter-group
  independence: the same operation is what makes a node's identity
  ambiguous here.

==============================================================================
  C6  identifiability: k and h are NOT separately recoverable
==============================================================================
  The kernel is exp(-k ||(x-c)/h||^2), and the operator fits the EXPONENT,
  i.e. alpha = k / h^2.  It follows that a wrong assumed k leaves alpha, the
  centre and the consistency relation EXACTLY right and scales the recovered
  width by sqrt(k_assumed / k_true).  This was found by a negative control
  that FAILED to fail, which is the useful kind of failure: the check cannot
  catch a wrong k because there is nothing there to catch.

   true k  assumed k    ctr err  h recovered/true  sqrt(k_a/k_t)     consist
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
      0.5        0.3   6.28e-07          0.774597       0.774597    3.39e-07
      0.3        0.5   8.03e-07          1.290994       1.290994    2.87e-06

  C6 prediction sqrt(k_a/k_t) confirmed        : PASS

  CONSEQUENCE, and it is a requirement on the hardware rather than a defect:
  the kernel coefficient k is a HARDWARE CONSTANT that must be KNOWN, not
  fitted.  The operator recovers a node's geometry only up to that constant;
  with k known -- which it is, k=0.5 for the narrow channel and k=0.3 for the
  wide one, both fixed by the bias network -- the width comes out exactly.

  The geometry operator is correct: it recovers a node's centre and width
  in closed form from the device's own readings, on both kernel channels
  and in 1-4 dimensions; its consistency relation holds for a genuine
  single node and is violated by a sum of two nodes and by pure noise.
  Two limits are MEASURED rather than assumed: partition-of-unity
  normalisation is detectable only when neither node dominates the probe
  region (so the check must be told which regime it is in), and only the
  combination alpha = k/h^2 is observable, which is why k must be a known
  hardware constant.

  A node network can therefore be trained on its GEOMETRY by an operator,
  not by search.  That is the other half of the closed-form pair, and it
  is the piece a 'network made of parameters' needs to be trainable with
  operators built for it instead of generic gradients.
