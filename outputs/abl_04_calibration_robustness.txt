  'hyb cost' = accumulated measurements the physically-calibrated arm had
  to spend to reach that frontier, relative to the search arms.  It wins
  while spending MORE, which is the conservative direction.

  READ THIS BEFORE COMPARING hyb_ideal WITH hyb_meas.  They are NOT the
  same training stage.  Because the x-axis is accumulated measurements
  and a physical calibration is charged at its full cost, the
  physically-calibrated arm reaches the frontier at an EARLIER epoch than
  the twin-calibrated one: it has prepaid its calibrations but done less
  finite-difference training.  Verified: at zero device noise the two
  calibrations produce BIT-IDENTICAL parameters (max diff 0.0), so any
  difference at the frontier is purely the epoch at which each arm gets
  there, and it makes the calibrated arm's win the conservative one.

  NOTE on the two axes.  sensor_noise corrupts the feature matrix the
  calibration solves with, so it is the axis that tests the CALIBRATION.
  position_resolution is a deterministic error already inside every
  forward, so ideal and measured features are identical on that axis by
  construction; it tests the DEVICE, not the calibration's readout.

  Physically-calibrated arm beats the best search arm at the common
  frontier in 8/8 device levels.

  HEADLINE SURVIVES, and survives while being charged for a physical
  calibration: at every device level tested the calibrated arm is
  ahead of every search arm at equal accumulated measurements, even
  though it had to spend ~2x the measurements to get there.
  Device readout noise at or above 1e-4 (relative to full scale)
  destroys finite-difference search -- with the probe step already
  adapted to the noise floor -- while leaving the calibrated arm
  essentially untouched.
