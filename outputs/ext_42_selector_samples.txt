  **THE CEILING RISES WITH THE SELECTION SET ON BOTH TASKS**, so the samples are
  doing something real.  **THE PURCHASE, THOUGH, IS TASK-DEPENDENT AND NOT A
  MAJORITY VOTE.**  Counting heads gives 'nodes win 5 of
  8', and an earlier draft of this verdict stopped there.  **That count
  is not a result**, for two reasons:

    * `steps` at n_sel = 102 is a NODE-ARM BLOW-UP: the node arm gains
      `-4.7381`, a geometry whose prediction explodes outside the basis
      support -- the failure mode `ext_21` named.  **Selection 'winning' there says
      nothing about selection.**
    * the two tasks disagree in a STABLE way: `4d` favours nodes at EVERY point while
      `steps` favours selection at three of four.  So the honest statement is
      **task-dependent**, and the uniform direction this extension usually reports
      does not hold here.

  **AND THE NODE ARM'S INSTABILITY IS ITSELF PART OF THE PRICE.**  Buying nodes buys a
  bigger geometry SEARCH, and this project has measured that the search can produce an
  exploding geometry; buying selection samples buys a better-posed CHOICE among
  whatever geometries the same search produced.  **So the comparison is not only
  accuracy per measurement, it is VARIANCE per measurement** -- and on `steps` the
  node arm's variance is visible in this very table.

  **SO THE GUARD SHOULD NOT BE BUILT -- AND FOR A BETTER REASON THAN 'IT DOES NOT
  PAY'.**  Three guards failed because they changed the statistic; the fourth lever,
  the samples, is the one the diagnostic named, and **it works: the oracle ceiling
  rises on both tasks, by `+0.59` and `+1.96`.**  What does not follow is that buying
  it beats buying nodes -- that is task-dependent, and the node arm's own instability
  is part of the accounting.

  **ITEM 2'S HONEST CLOSURE: the shipped rule is defensible neither as accurate nor
  as improvable by guarding, but as the most STABLE option per measurement** -- which
  is what `ext_22` concluded, now with the one untried direction MEASURED rather than
  assumed.  **Item 2's candidate repairs -- three guards, a criterion replacement,
  and more samples -- now have measured answers, and only the samples raise anything.**

  **THE LIMITS.**  The oracle here is a CEILING on what a selector could do with that
  set, not a selector -- it re-solves every strength for every candidate and is
  itself an overfitting risk at small `n_sel`, which is exactly why it cannot be
  shipped.  What is measured is whether more samples RAISE that ceiling, which they
  do, and whether buying them beats buying nodes, which they do not.  Repository
  tasks, 40 nodes, 3 seeds, toy.
