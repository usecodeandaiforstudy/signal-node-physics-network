  The trainer plans ENTIRELY inside the twin: placement, features and the
  gate's held-out score all use the twin's kernel.  Unlike the base version's
  calibration -- which can be performed on the device by reading Z through
  the sensor chain -- the operator trainer has NO device-side signal, so a
  twin error should be invisible to its gate.  That is the prediction tested
  here, and it is the one result that could bound the physical claim.

==================================================================================
  VERDICT: is the operator trainer protected against a wrong twin?
==================================================================================

  P1a wrong CHANNEL: self-deception +0.0155
  P1b wrong WIDTH  : device loss as planned -0.1660, after device re-calibration -0.1890
  P2  placement    : device loss as planned -0.0173, after device re-calibration -0.0166
  largest self-deception overall: +0.1660

  NOT PROTECTED BY THE TWIN, exactly as predicted: the twin's own report
  disagrees with what the device delivers, and it disagrees in the
  FLATTERING direction, because the twin's features are the ones the
  strengths were fitted to.  The operator trainer's gate cannot see this:
  placement, features and validation all use the same wrong kernel, so
  there is nothing in the loop for the error to contradict.

  AND THE GEOMETRY DOES NOT SURVIVE: device re-calibration does not
  recover the loss, so the twin error has damaged the placement itself.
  That is the outcome that BOUNDS the physical claim, and it must be
  reported as such -- with the repair, which exists and is already
  verified:

    the operator trainer plans its geometry in the twin and, unlike the
    base version's calibration, has NO device-side reading in the loop.
    Re-solving strengths where they are used is not enough, because the
    error is in the BASIS rather than in the weights.  The repair has to
    happen one level up: MEASURE the realized geometry -- which ext_09
    showed is exact at the minimum probe count, recovered/true = 1.000 --
    and re-plan with what the device actually has.  Until that loop is
    closed, the operator trainer's geometry is conditional on the twin,
    and no amount of device-side strength calibration fixes it.

  AN ASYMMETRY worth carrying into any hardware budget: the damage is one-
  sided.  Realized widths NARROWER than planned are what hurt (at -20% the
  device falls to +0.4157 while the twin still reports +0.5817), whereas
  wider-than-planned sometimes scores slightly BETTER than exact (+0.6216 at
  +10%).  A placement tolerance should therefore be specified on the narrow
  side rather than symetrically.
