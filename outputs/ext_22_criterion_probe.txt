  ext_19 measured that on `steps` the rule's proposals win only 42.5% of grow
  steps against an EQUAL-SIZED random pool (chance ~50%), while on `4d` they win
  63.3%.  That points at the CRITERION rather than the pool, because both pools
  were ranked BY THE SAME CRITERION -- if it does not track the outcome, two
  pools of different quality look identical through it.  This tests that
  directly instead of assuming it.

  crit      = ||res||^2 - ||res - w_j z_j||^2   (training set, ONE node, before
              the strengths are re-solved) -- the shipped rule
  val_after = held-out R^2 after adding the candidate and re-solving EVERY
              strength -- what actually matters

==========================================================================================
  P1  does the greedy criterion track what actually happens?
==========================================================================================

  task       steps   rank agreement   mean regret   regret / spread
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  steps        120           +0.097        0.0415             0.464
  4d           120           +0.191        0.0513             0.545

  'rank agreement' is the correlation between the criterion's ranking of the
  candidates and their ranking by the held-out score AFTER re-solving the
  strengths.  'regret' is the held-out R^2 the criterion's pick leaves on the
  table versus the best candidate available, and 'regret / spread' expresses it
  against the spread of held-out scores in the pool.

  MY HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED, AND WHAT REPLACES IT IS A BETTER ANSWER.
  The criterion does NOT track the outcome on EITHER task (+0.191 on
  the SMOOTH control against +0.097 on the discontinuous one), so this
  is not a property of discontinuous targets at all -- it is a general
  weakness of ranking by a one-node, training-set reduction, on both tasks.

  AND THE REGRET SAYS HOW MUCH IT COSTS: the criterion's pick leaves about
  HALF the pool's spread on the table (0.46 of the spread on
  `steps`, 0.54 on `4d`).  So there IS a better candidate
  available at almost every step and the rule does not find it -- which is
  the mechanism behind ext_19's 'random matches the rule' that does NOT
  require the pool to be bad.

  BUT REPLACING IT IS CATASTROPHIC, and the way it fails is the finding:
  the re-solve arm reaches val +0.8412 /
  +0.9098 while its TEST scores are
  -0.1927 / -1.2580 -- a val-to-test gap of
  +1.034 and +2.168.
  Ranking ~70 candidates per step on a 51-sample split for 40 steps is a
  great deal of selection on very little data, so it OVERFITS the validation
  set far worse than the shipped rule underfits the outcome.  This is the
  risk stated before the run, and it materialised.

  SO THE ANSWER IS NEITHER 'THE CANDIDATES' NOR 'THE CRITERION' -- IT IS A
  BIAS-VARIANCE TRADE, AND THE SAMPLE COUNT IS WHAT BINDS.  The shipped rule
  is a LOW-VARIANCE estimator (a one-node training-set reduction) that is
  biased; the replacement is closer to unbiased and far noisier.  With 51
  validation samples the biased one wins, and it wins by a wide margin --
  which makes the shipped choice DEFENSIBLE for a reason this document had
  never given: not because it is accurate, but because it is stable.

  WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THE UPSTREAM ITEM: it is not fixable by changing the
  ranking rule at this scale.  The binding constraint is the SAMPLE COUNT,
  which is a property of the toy setup (256 train / 51 validation) and not
  of the method.  Testing that would mean raising the sample count and
  re-running this probe, and until then the honest state is that the defect
  has been LOCATED rather than repaired -- and located somewhere the
  previous three experiments did not point at.
