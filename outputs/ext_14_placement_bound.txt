  best-found is an UPPER bound on achievable R^2, so the greedy-vs-best gap
  is a LOWER bound on the true gap.  A small gap rules out the interesting
  failure (greedy being badly suboptimal) at this scale; it does not prove
  optimality, and this report does not claim it does.

  capture = (G - R) / (B - R): the fraction of the available improvement the
  current rule obtains.  1.0 means no restart did better; 0.5 would mean half
  the achievable gain is being missed.

  WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY, in order of how much they matter:

  1. AT THE OPERATING POINT THE RULE IS NEARLY OPTIMAL BY THIS MEASURE.
     Every other half-B result runs at N=40, where capture is 0.976
     and the gap to best-found is only -0.0113.  So this bound does NOT
     undercut the headline results: at the node count they use, a single
     greedy run obtains essentially everything any restart finds.

  2. THE RULE IS MYOPIC AT SMALL N, and the measurement says how much.
     Capture falls to 0.282 at N=4 -- where random
     geometry (+0.0729) actually BEATS greedy (+0.0982),
     because with very few nodes each greedy choice locks in a bad
     configuration that the remaining nodes cannot repair.  The trend is
     monotone in N: 4:0.28, 8:0.62, 16:0.73, 40:0.98.
     This is the bound ext_09 could not supply, and it bounds the method from
     BELOW -- at small N, which is worth knowing before anyone scales it down.

  3. The CANDIDATE POOL IS NOT THE LIMITATION.  Widening it from 4 random
     candidates to 32 moves the score by less than 0.05 at every node
     count, while merely choosing a different RESTART by validation is
     worth +0.022 to +0.067.
     So the gap is run-to-run VARIATION, not a shortage of proposals --
     and that has a cheap fix, which is the actionable part of this result:

        run M restarts and keep the one with the best HELD-OUT score

     That is the base version's gate discipline applied to the placement,
     it costs M times a training run that takes a fraction of a second, and
     it captures most of the gap at small N without touching the proposal
     rule at all.

  STATED AS A LIMIT, because it is one: best-found is an upper bound on what
  is achievable, so the true gap may be larger than measured and never
  smaller.  A node family other than Gaussians, or an optimiser other than
  restarts of this one, could do better and would not be detected here.

  For the record, a test-set peek would have bought up to +0.0577
  more than the honest validation-picked baseline.  It is NOT used, and the
  size of that number is the reason the distinction is worth stating.
