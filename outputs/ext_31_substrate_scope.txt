  The operators rest on ONE identity, and the extension's own section 11 item 5
  says the widest claim in the document -- 'any physical system' -- is the least
  covered, because both substrates tested were built to reproduce the SAME
  engineered Gaussian kernel.  That is a circularity, not just a gap.

  THE PRECONDITION IS NOT 'GAUSSIAN'.  It is: **log z(x) must be LINEAR in some
  fixed, FINITE basis phi(x) of the input** -- that is what makes the coefficients
  identifiable from a measured field.  The Gaussian satisfies it because its
  exponent is a degree-2 polynomial in x.  ANY polynomial exponent does.

  basis degrees tried: [2, 4, 6, 8]  (monomials of total degree <= d)

========================================================================================================

  'in scope' is the PRIOR -- whether the exponent is a polynomial in x.  The
  residual is the MEASUREMENT, and the two must agree; where they do, the
  residual is a usable test on a substrate nobody has characterised.

========================================================================================================
  P2  the substrate-specific step: recovering the SOURCE from the coefficients
========================================================================================================

  The basis is what a new substrate changes; the INVERSION has to be re-derived
  per substrate.  For a polynomial exponent of degree d the top-degree
  coefficient fixes the width and the degree-(d-1) coefficients fix the position:
      d=2 :  coef(x_j^2) = -alpha          coef(x_j)   = +2*alpha*c_j
      d=4 :  coef(x_j^4) = -(k/h^4)        coef(x_j^3) = +4*c_j*(k/h^4)

  substrate               basis d                c recovered   h recovered    c error
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  gaussian (control)            2      [ 0.700000 -0.400000]      1.300000   1.67e-15
  gaussian (control)            4 (inversion not defined here)
  quartic exponent              2      [ 0.778201 -0.416144]      0.496421   7.82e-02
  quartic exponent              4      [ 0.700000 -0.400000]      1.300000   2.00e-15

  A basis that is too SMALL cannot represent the identity, so the recovery there
  is meaningless -- AND THE CODE STILL RETURNS A NUMBER.  That is visible above:
  on the quartic substrate read through the DEGREE-2 basis the position comes back
  as a plausible-looking but wrong point and the width as roughly a third of the
  true one.  **An out-of-scope basis does not fail loudly; it answers confidently
  and wrongly.**  So the residual is not a nicety -- it has to be checked BEFORE
  any position is believed, and that is the whole argument for P4.

  AND WHERE THE BASIS IS RIGHT, THE GENERALISATION IS EXACT: the quartic substrate
  read through the DEGREE-4 basis returns the source to machine precision, just as
  the Gaussian does through degree 2.  **What a new substrate changes is the basis;
  the inversion is re-derived with it, and neither is a matter of luck.**

========================================================================================================
  P4  THE DELIVERABLE: the residual as a qualification test for a new substrate
========================================================================================================

  Put a measured field through the fit and read the residual.  A substrate is in
  scope iff the residual COLLAPSES at some finite degree -- so a substrate nobody
  has characterised can be qualified BEFORE any operator is trusted on it.

  gaussian (control)     residual says IN SCOPE     prior says IN           AGREE
  quartic exponent       residual says IN SCOPE     prior says IN           AGREE
  yukawa exp(-kr)/r      residual says OUT OF SCOPE prior says OUT          AGREE
  cauchy 1/(1+(r/g)^2)   residual says OUT OF SCOPE prior says OUT          AGREE

  prior and measurement agree on 4/4 substrates.

  THE PRECONDITION IS THE RIGHT ONE.  'Any physical system' narrows to a
  CHECKABLE statement -- any system whose per-source response is log-linear in
  a fixed finite basis -- and the check is a least-squares residual, not a
  judgement call.  **A polynomial-exponent substrate is IN SCOPE and the
  operators generalise to it by swapping the basis; a Yukawa or Lorentzian
  field is OUT and the residual says so before anything is built on it.**

  SO THE THESIS'S WIDEST CLAIM IS NOT REFUTED, IT IS BOUNDED: the operators
  need log-linearity in a finite basis, which is a property a substrate can be
  TESTED for rather than assumed.  That is strictly more than the two-Gaussian-
  cells evidence supported, and strictly less than 'any physical system'.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO: the operators themselves are not re-derived here, only
  the IDENTIFICATION PREMISE they rest on.  Section 7.1's optimality bound was
  measured for the Gaussian basis and is NOT transferred to the quartic one.
  And nothing here is a device: these are profiles evaluated at points, which is
  the right level for a question about which responses are admissible, and the
  wrong level for claiming anything about hardware.
