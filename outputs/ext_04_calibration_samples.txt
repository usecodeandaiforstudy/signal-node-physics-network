  Readout chosen on a validation slice of the calibration set, never on
  test.  Learned combination only: ext_02 refuted the frozen +-1 code, and a
  refuted arm would only obscure the curve.

  MEASUREMENTS, on the record before the curve: under the base version's own
  cost model the calibration costs n_train * sum_k N_k = n_fit * 40 for
  EVERY form below.  The rank constraint therefore does NOT reduce physical
  measurements; it reduces PARAMETERS, which buys conditioning.  This script
  reports both so the two cannot be confused.

  NO crossover at the sizes tested: the rank-constrained readout never
  beats the full-rank one by more than the run-to-run spread.  The saving
  is then a pure parameter saving -- still real (the hardware stores and
  tunes fewer coefficients) but it does NOT improve calibration quality,
  and it must not be reported as if it did.

  Either way: the CALIBRATION MEASUREMENT count is unchanged.  Any claim of
  a measurement saving from the rank constraint would be wrong under the
  project's own cost model, and this run is the evidence for saying so.

  DO NOT MISREAD THE RATIO COLUMN.  This sweep reaches 20x readout
  parameters per calibration sample and the full-rank readout still
  calibrates (+0.41 at 20x).  That does NOT contradict the base version's
  measured failure boundary at 2.19x, for two reasons that must travel with
  these numbers: (1) the geometry here is FIXED, whereas the arm that failed
  in the base version also SEARCHED its geometry by finite differences, and
  a badly scaled readout corrupts that search; (2) the ridge strength here is
  chosen on validation per arm, which is itself the protection the base
  version had to add after its own first attempt failed.  The transferable
  statement is the shape of the curve, not the absolute ratio at which it
  turns over.
