  ext_43 - the closed-form calibration, inside the deep trainer
  epochs=60 seeds=3 particles=20
  2026-10-06 11:31:29
==================================================================

  The project's largest single claim is that the strengths should be CALIBRATED in
  closed form, not searched (FINDINGS.md section 6: 15-30x fewer measurements).  And
  the deep paths do not do it: `training/himal_nd.py` runs per-parameter FD / SPSA on
  EVERY parameter, and the CIFAR line runs autograd.  **On the project's own deepest
  demonstration the strengths are still searched.**

  SCOPE, CHECKED BEFORE ACTING: `HiMALND`'s REGRESSION path is
  `out = (hA + hB)/2` with `h = z @ W^T` -- LINEAR in the strengths -- and its own
  docstring says it is STAGEWISE RESIDUAL ('L2/L3 learn y - accumulated_pred'),
  which is the structure `solve_readout_closed_form` is built on.  Its
  CLASSIFICATION path is nearest-prototype voting and is NOT linear, so the
  calibration does not apply there -- that is the case DESIGN.md's table already
  marks 'readout linear in strengths? no'.

  AND A SIMPLIFICATION FOUND ON THE WAY: `_forward_layer` returns `out_g + out_a`,
  i.e. `z @ (W_gauss + W_act)^T`.  **The two channels are DEGENERATE -- only their
  sum matters** -- so the calibration solves the sum and the other channel is zeroed.
  Solving only one and leaving the other would ADD the old weights back on the next
  forward and undo the solve; that mistake is written down here because it is silent.

====================================================================================================
  P1  the cost share, from the parameter layout
====================================================================================================

  `get_params_vector` = centres (D*N) + h (N) + W_gauss (n_vars*N) + W_act (n_vars*N).
  Every one of those is perturbed twice per FD step, so the share of the search's
  budget spent on the READOUT is:

   n_vars  readout share of the parameter vector
  ------------------------------------------------
        1                                 33.3%
        2                                 50.0%
        3                                 60.0%
        4                                 66.7%

  **A third of the search's work at `n_vars = 1`, and it is work the calibration does
  exactly.**  That is what the wrapper reclaims, and the arm it is tested against
  keeps spending it -- so the comparison below is CONSERVATIVE.

====================================================================================================
  P3  VERDICT
====================================================================================================

  **CONTROL FIRST: the shipped SEARCH arm averages +0.2291 against the
  project's own 0.05 floor -- it LEARNS, so the comparison is meaningful.**

  calibration minus search, per seed: +0.0357, -0.0016, -0.2469
  mean -0.0710, calibration better on 1 of 3 seeds

  **THE CALIBRATION DOES NOT HELP HERE**, on the same forward budget and with
  the comparison stacked in its favour.  That is a NEGATIVE answer to the
  worklist's item 1 and it is worth as much as a positive one: it would mean the
  deep path's strengths are not the bottleneck, and the search is earning its
  forwards on the GEOMETRY rather than on the readout.

  **WHAT THE WRAPPER DOES NOT FIX, and it is the honest limit.**  The calibration
  is applied AFTER the SPSA step, so the search still moves the readout and the
  calibration then corrects it; a true 'calibrate, do not search' arm would remove
  those coordinates from the parameter vector, which is a change to
  `get_params_vector` -- a BASE method -- and is NOT done here.  **The measurement
  therefore bounds the gain from below rather than measuring it.**

  **WHAT THE PER-LAYER SOLVE SHOWS, which is why the answer is negative rather than
  disappointing.**  The solve fits its own residual target to `R^2 ~ 0.34-0.38` on
  every seed, so the calibration WORKS per layer -- and it still does not improve the
  end-to-end result.  **So the readout is not the bottleneck of the deep path, and the
  search is earning its forwards on the GEOMETRY.**  That is a negative answer to
  worklist item 1, and it is the answer the measurement supports.

  **AND THE CALIBRATION CAN HURT: seed 2 is `-0.2469`.**  The wrapper overwrites the
  searched readout with a ridge solve on a held-out slice of the layer's OWN data, and
  when that slice is unrepresentative the replacement is worse than what the search
  had.  With one seed in three materially negative, the honest reading is that the
  calibration is not RELIABLY better here, not merely that it fails to be better.

  **WHAT CHANGED BETWEEN THIS RUN AND THE PREVIOUS ONE, because it is the context that
  makes this measurement mean anything.**  The first run of this script could not
  compare at all: the SEARCH arm scored `-0.0341` and did not clear the floor, so the
  control refused.  `ext_44` then showed WHY -- `HiMALND.predict`'s regression branch
  returned the last layer's raw output, which is a RESIDUAL, instead of the
  accumulation `train` builds -- and that defect was FIXED in the base (12.29).  **So
  this is the first comparison ever made on the deep regression path, and the first
  time that path has cleared the project's own learning floor.**

  **AND THE COST SIDE IS STILL OPEN.**  A calibration costs `n_train * N` measurements
  under the base's own model (`readout_calibration_cost`), which is not free -- 12.21
  and 12.27 both measured that the same measurements often buy more elsewhere.  Here
  the arm that pays that cost does not even win on accuracy, so the question does not
  need asking; on a task where it DID win, it would.
