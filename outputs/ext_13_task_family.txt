  WHAT THE ADVERSARIES ACTUALLY SHOWED, read off this table rather than

    steps   OPERATOR -0.0297   RANDOM +0.0573   FD -0.0138
    hetero  OPERATOR +0.2303   RANDOM +0.0730   FD -0.0428

  `steps` DOES break it: the operator arm is the WORST of the three on
  the discontinuous target, and random geometry beats it.  The
  Gaussian basis cannot represent a square wave, and a constructor that
  keeps reducing the residual spends its nodes chasing the
  discontinuity.  This is the adversary working as designed.

  `hetero` does NOT break it: the operator arm is the best of the
  three there, and more than reverses the FD arm's negative score.

  SO THE ADVERSARY RESULT IS SPLIT, one each -- which is a better answer
  than either uniform outcome would have been, and it is only visible
  because the seed count was raised until the comparison could resolve.

  AND THE 3-SEED READING WAS WRONG.  At 3 seeds this table said the
  operator arm was the BEST on `steps` (+0.155 against +0.073 and -0.018)
  and that it did NOT fit pure noise hardest.  At 12 seeds it is the WORST
  on `steps` (-0.030 against +0.057) and it DOES fit the noise hardest.
  Both flips go AGAINST the method, and both were forecast as 'more seeds
  would settle it' -- so this is the settling, not a new experiment.

  A PREDICTION THAT FAILED, recorded because it was stated in advance: the
  design note argued that `shift` would be SHARPER for half B than for half
  A, since the operator trainer plans entirely inside the twin and validates
  in-distribution, so it should be structurally blind to a shift.  It is not
  -- the operator arm scores +0.1249 against +0.0309 and
  +0.0520, i.e. four times the alternatives.
  The reason is that half B's PLACEMENT is not gate-driven: the gate only
  decides whether to keep a refinement round, and ext_09 measured that step to
  be inert.  So the blindness argument applies to the base version's
  gate-driven calibration and NOT to a constructor that reduces the residual
  directly.  The prediction was wrong for a reason worth keeping.
