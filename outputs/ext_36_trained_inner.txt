  The extension's section 11, item 1: the trade is measured and the cheap form
  says keep the linearity, but 'W and b were drawn, not trained ... a TRAINED
  inner layer is the untested case'.  ext_18 called its own result a LOWER bound
  for the same reason.  This runs the untested case.

  THE COST IS NOT AMBIGUOUS, and it was checked against the design document
  before running: DESIGN.md's table marks a nonlinear-in-the-readings combination
  as NOT linear in the STRENGTHS, because a scaling coefficient IS a source's
  excitation strength.  So the inner matrix is a strength matrix that must be
  SEARCHED, and it is charged as one:
      L40       n_train * 40   closed form
      N(80)   2 * (80*40 + 80) * 5 = 32,800 probes   searched
      L320     n_train * 320   closed form

  THE COST, UNDER BOTH ACCOUNTINGS, because the two are different things and
  the conclusion should not depend on which one is chosen:

    (a) CALIBRATION-style, `n_train` measurements per strength -- what a closed-
        form calibration costs, and what `ext_18` charged every arm including the
        nonlinear one.
    (b) SEARCH-style, the probe count -- what searching costs, and what the design
        document's own framing implies for a layer whose strengths CANNOT be
        calibrated in closed form ('they must be SEARCHED').

    arm                 strengths    (a) calib   (b) search   R2 @ (a)   R2 @ (b)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    L40                        40       10,240       10,240    +3.1083    +3.1083
    N drawn H=80            3,280      839,680       20,480    +0.0406    +1.6650
    N trained H=80          3,280      839,680       32,800    +0.0375    +0.9590
    L320                      320       81,920       81,920    +0.5434    +0.5434

  **THE TWO ACCOUNTINGS DISAGREE ABOUT THE NONLINEAR ARM AND AGREE ABOUT THE
  VERDICT.**  `ext_18` charged it `n_train * strengths` -- the CALIBRATION cost --
  for a layer the design document says cannot be calibrated, only searched.  Under
  (b) the trained arm is actually CHEAPER in probes than the node arm at these
  settings (32,800 against 81,920), because a search evaluates the loss
  rather than measuring every strength.  **So the cost objection to the nonlinear
  arm is weaker than `ext_18`'s table implies.**

  **AND UNDER (b) THE DRAWN ARM WINS PER MEASUREMENT, which is a qualification
  `ext_18` could not see because it charged every arm the same way.**  Read the
  last column: the drawn nonlinear arm returns a HIGHER R^2 per 1e5 measurements
  than the node arm, because its `W` is drawn and therefore free -- only the outer
  values are calibrated, against the node arm's 320.  **So the trade's conclusion
  is NOT accounting-independent: at a fixed ACCURACY the node arm wins, and under a
  SEARCH-style charge at a fixed BUDGET the drawn nonlinear arm wins.**

  That does not overturn `ext_18` -- its framing is accuracy at a given budget and
  under its own accounting it is right -- but it means 'keep the linearity' is a
  statement about ACCURACY rather than about efficiency.  **Which accounting the
  hardware actually pays is a modelling question this script surfaces and does not
  settle**: it depends on whether the inner matrix is PROGRAMMED (free, like a
  digital weight) or MEASURED (charged, like a source strength), and the design
  document says the nonlinear layer's strengths cannot be CALIBRATED -- which is an
  argument that they are not measured.

  **AND THE TRAINING MADE IT WORSE, not merely not-better.**  The average
  change is -0.0264 -- a LOSS -- so the drawn inner layer was not a
  handicap that training removes; on this schedule it was the better choice.
  That is worth stating plainly rather than as 'a real but insufficient gain'.

  **THE TRADE SURVIVES THE UNTESTED CASE, ON ACCURACY.**  Training the inner
  layer changes the result by -0.0264, and the node arm still wins on every
  task.  **So ext_18's lower-bound caveat was the right caveat and it does not
  change the conclusion**, which is the one thing section 11 item 1 said could
  overturn it.

  **BUT NOT ON EFFICIENCY, and that qualifier is load-bearing.**  An earlier
  draft of this verdict said the node arm wins 'at a fraction of the
  measurements', which is TRUE under the calibration-style charge and FALSE
  under the search-style one -- the two accountings are reported side by side
  above precisely so that a sentence like that cannot stand unqualified.

  REPRODUCIBILITY BOUND, measured rather than assumed: two runs of this script
  with identical arguments agreed to four decimals but not bit for bit.  Every
  draw here IS seeded (`fit_operator`, `nonlinear_readout`, `trained_inner` all
  take a seed), so the difference is float-level nondeterminism in the linear
  algebra -- the same class as the base trainer's missing seed but much milder:
  it is the SAME draw computed slightly differently, not a different draw.  **A
  conclusion that turns on the fourth decimal should not be read from this table.**

  SCOPE: N=40 node set from the operator trainer, H=80, 5 block-coordinate epochs, 3 seeds, three tasks, toy.
  The training scheme is ONE choice among several; a longer schedule or a
  different parameterisation could buy more, and nothing here bounds that.
