  THE MECHANISM OF THE FAILURE: a restart can build a pathological geometry
  whose held-out R^2 looks fine while its weights are huge, and a Gaussian
  basis that decays outside its support, MULTIPLIED BY A LARGE WEIGHT, explodes
  there.  The base version documented exactly this on its `shift` task.  So
  held-out R^2 ALONE cannot select among restarts, and `restarts=M` as
  implemented is a fix that reaches the ceiling at some M and is actively
  dangerous at others -- reported as a ceiling reached AND a selector that
  needs a guard, not as 'the failure is closed'.

  P2 on `steps` the rule's best candidate wins 42.5% of grow steps against
  an equal-sized random pool (chance is ~50%); on `4d` it wins 63.3%.

  SO THE PROPOSAL FAMILY IS AT CHANCE ON THE DISCONTINUOUS TASK.  Not WORSE
  than random -- an earlier unbalanced version of this probe claimed that by
  comparing ~5 rule candidates against 32 random draws -- but no better than
  guessing either.  That answers the mechanism question in part: on `steps`
  the log-linear residual profile stops being informative, so restarts are a
  lottery over proposals that carry no signal, while on the smooth control
  the same rule wins clearly.  It also explains why the repair is UNSTABLE
  there rather than merely imperfect.
