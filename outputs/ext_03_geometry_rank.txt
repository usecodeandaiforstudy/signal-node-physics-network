==============================================================================
  VERDICT: does the rank constraint still cost nothing once the geometry
  is trained?
==============================================================================

  geometry movement at the end: 0.0699 relative (floor 0.05) -> MOVED
  full-rank arm (k=8): final test R^2 +0.7404 (sd 0.0328 ACROSS SEEDS) at 1.67x readout/samples

  PAIRED comparison.  The arms see the SAME data seeds, so the correct
  statistic is the per-seed difference (k minus full), not the difference
  of means against the across-seed sd -- the latter is dominated by which
  data split was drawn and makes the test unable to fail.
  k=4: paired gap -0.0002 (sd 0.0008, sem 0.0004 over 5 seeds)  tol 0.0200  OK
  k=3: paired gap -0.0027 (sd 0.0031, sem 0.0014 over 5 seeds)  tol 0.0200  OK
  k=2: paired gap -0.0357 (sd 0.0091, sem 0.0041 over 5 seeds)  tol 0.0200  FAILS

  boundary: largest tested k < m is 4; smallest PASSING k is 3
  -> the affordable region is k >= 3; failing k = 2

  Does the constraint's cost GROW as the geometry is trained?  A paired gap
  that widens would mean the geometry and the rank constraint compete; one
  that stays flat or narrows means they do not.

   epoch      k=4 gap     k=3 gap     k=2 gap
  ------------------------------------------
       1      +0.0000     -0.0024     -0.0311
      31      -0.0004     -0.0029     -0.0369
      60      -0.0002     -0.0027     -0.0357

  SURVIVES DOWN TO A BOUNDARY.  The larger rank-constrained readouts keep
  full-rank accuracy while the geometry is trained against them, and the
  smaller ones do not -- which is the USEFUL outcome, because it locates
  the design point instead of only confirming that one exists.
  Affordable region: k >= 3.  Combined with ext_02 (which found
  the same boundary at a fixed geometry) the conclusion is that the
  boundary is a property of the ARCHITECTURE, not of the untrained
  geometry: geometry training neither widens nor narrows the affordable
  region.
